## 7. Trying to be a Hero
- Don't assume you're right about everything and optimize to the best case scenario. 
- Plan for worst case scenario. Try to set things so you're likely to make money even when you're wrong. 
- Make money if I'm kind of right, don't loose too much money if I'm wrong. 
- More rules, and more parameters you add to a trading strategy the more brittle its like to be, and more less likely it is to be profitable in wide range of future outcomes that you can't predict. 
- Overly specific implementation of a good idea can loose money even if it is a good idea sound. 
- Effective trading strategies tend to be very simple. Concentrate to play the simple games in the right place. 
- Where can i find desperate buyers, desperate sellers, so i can buy low sell high. 
- How do I manage risk I take on doing that?
- How can I do that in an environment that is as uncompetitive as possible?
- Keep it simple, keep it believable, and always assume things are not going to be as you expect them to be. 
- Give yourself chance to make money if you are wrong.

### 9 Edge, Expectation & Luck
- A good trade is not the one that makes money;  A bad trade is not the one that loses money. "A good trade is one with positive expectation (too cheap or too rich)."
- We can easily get the trades that are wrong, but its harder to get the trades that are right. 

### 10. Competition
- Edges in the market; some can be highly competitive but mostly uncompetitive.
- To make money, you need someone to be prepared to trade with you at "wrong" price.
- You are in competition with other traders, and you need to be able to take advantage of it in competitive environment. 
- The immense competition means two things --
	- Its hard to make money trading, unless you pick your battles carefully.
	- It's hard to make bad buy & sell decisions because you get to trade at prices set by smartest in the business.
### Notes; Case Study
- What can we do to make money today, given our existing tool and resources. 
- Understand the market, find the discrepancy, build a hypothesis, test it - form market edge.
- Your worst day is not in your back test, its in your future, and it makes good sense to prepare for the best you can.  --- Sizing things sensibly, and trading lots of different things. 
- Always ask yourself why you can harness a particular edge given that you are not the most competitive player. Focus on areas where its likely you can actually compete. 
- Focus on understanding the edge, not building a trading strategy. 
- The greatest edge as a systematic trader is the ability to trade uncorrelated edges together. 
- Try to focus on bigger picture (portfolio), rather than agonizing about the performance of individual strategies.
- Why would you get paid in the markets?
- Research, not back test. Focus on making money today, rather than time building for future or solving problems you do not have. 
- Be slow at the start, get feedback from the market and set up important processes while you make mistakes that won't cost as much as if you were more gung ho.
- There will be more things to trade. Eventually, you will have more things to trade than you can possibly manage alone.
- Don't pass up easy trades because they are not sexy.
- Risk premia harvesting positions is a good pace to start at an appropriate pace. 
- On new strategies, do the simplest thing first. Using lowest frequency data you can, favoring ETF/Fund data over derivatives for your initial analysis where possible. If that idea does not stack up with the simplest approach, its unlikely to work out. 
- Precision is unrealistic, trying to impose it is wasted effort. Trading is more about being roughly at the right place at the right time. 
- Doing good Data Analysis, and weighing up uncertain evidence are useful skills regardless of how successful your trading is.
- Trading multiple mediocre edges has an extraordinary effect at the portfolio level
- Focus on what you can control -- exposure, good research, setting up & following good processes. 

- The key point is that while volatility increases linearly with size, total returns do not. And risk adjusted returns will decrease with size. 
- ***If you have a volatile instrument that returns 10% over some number of time periods, and you double the size of it, you get less than 20% returns.***
- As you keep on sizing up a volatile asset, you get diminishing returns, to the point where increasing size actually decreases returns.
