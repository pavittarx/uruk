# INTRO: Ratio Analysis
**Ratio analysis** is a quantitative method of analyzing a company's financial performance by examining the relationships between different pieces of financial information found in the company's financial statements. The primary objective of ratio analysis is to gain insights into a company's profitability, solvency, operational efficiency, and liquidity position. 

In simpler terms, it helps stakeholders understand:
- Operational Efficiency: How well a company is using its resources.
- Profitability: Its ability to generate profits and repay its debts.
- Liquidity & Solvency Position: Its overall financial health and stability.

Various stakeholders, including shareholders, creditors, suppliers, managers, employees, tax authorities, and the government, utilize ratio analysis to understand a company's financial performance.

Ratio analysis instead of focusing on plain numbers, examines the **proportions** and **relationships** between these numbers. This approach allows for a more meaningful interpretation of the data, revealing trends, strengths, and weaknesses that might not be immediately apparent from the raw figures alone.

The following financial statements are used in ratio analysis —

1. Profit & Loss A/c
2. Balance Sheet
3. Profit & Loss Appropriation A/c
4. Fund Flow Statement
5. Cash Flow Statement

- Ratios are used as a bench mark for evaluating the financial statements and performance of a firm.
- Ratios help stakeholders summarize large quantities of financial information (data). The ratio basically reflect quantitative relationship among different variables.
- Ratio are useless on their own, and are useful only if compared with some standard. The comparison standard can consist of — past ratios, competitor ratios, market leader ratios, industry ratios.

## Ratio Types 

### **1. Solvency/Leverage Ratios (Long Term Ratios)**
It determines whether a company has sufficient cash flow to manage obligations that are due.
- These are also called **leverage ratios.**
- A company with low solvency ratio is risk of not being able to fulfil its debt obligation, and is likely to default in debt repayment.
- It varies w/ type of industry, but 0.5 is a good measure.

- **Debt-Equity Ratio:** This ratio measures the proportion of debt and equity used to finance a company's assets. A higher ratio indicates higher financial leverage and potentially higher risk.

- **Debt-to-Asset Ratio:** Similar to the debt-equity ratio, this ratio measures the proportion of a company's assets financed by debt. A higher ratio suggests higher financial risk.

- **Interest Coverage Ratio (Times Interest Earned):** This ratio assesses a company's ability to cover its interest expense with its earnings before interest and taxes (EBIT).    

- **Fixed Charge Coverage Ratio:** A more comprehensive solvency ratio, it measures a company's ability to meet all its fixed financing charges, including interest expense and loan repayments.
	- **EBIT** - earnings before taxes, calculated by adding tax and interest expenses back to your net income
	- **Fixed Charges** - Recurring business expenses that are paid regardless of sales volumes, such as debt and lease expenses
	
| Ratio                                           | Formula                                                                                           |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Debt to Equity Ratio                            | $\dfrac{\text{Debt}}{\text{Equity}}$                                                              |
| Debt to Asset Ratio                             | $\dfrac{\text{Debt}}{\text{Assets}}$                                                              |
| Interest Coverage Ratio / Times Interest Earned | $\dfrac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Interest}}$                                                            |
| Fixed Charge Coverage Ratio                     | $\dfrac{\text{EBIT + Fixed Charges before taxes}}{ \text{Interest + Fixed Charges before taxes}}$ |

### 2. Liquidity Ratios (Short Term Solvency Ratios)
- **Current Ratio:** This ratio measures a company's ability to pay its short-term liabilities with its short-term assets. A higher current ratio indicates a stronger liquidity position.
- **Quick Ratio (Acid Test Ratio):** Similar to the current ratio, but it excludes inventory from current assets since inventory is considered less liquid.
- **Cash Ratio:** An even more stringent measure of liquidity, this ratio only considers cash and cash equivalents as a proportion of current liabilities.

| Ratio                         | Formula                                                                                   |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Current Ratio                 | $\dfrac{\text{Current Assets}}{\text{Current Liabilities}}$                               |
| Acid Test Ratio / Quick Ratio | $\dfrac{\text{Current Assets - Inventory}}{\text{Current Liabilities}}$                   |
| Cash Ratio                    | $\dfrac{(\text{Cash \& Bank Balances + Curr. Investments}) }{\text{Current Liabilities}}$ |

### 3. Activity/Turnover Ratios

These ratios measure how efficiently a company utilizes its assets.

**Inventory Turnover Ratio:** This ratio measures how quickly a company sells its inventory. A higher ratio indicates faster inventory turnover, which can be a sign of efficient inventory management.

**Debtors Turnover Ratio (Accounts Receivable Turnover):** This ratio measures how effectively a company collects its receivables. A higher ratio indicates faster collection and better credit management.
    
**Fixed Asset Turnover Ratio:** This ratio measures how efficiently a company uses its fixed assets to generate sales. A higher ratio suggests better utilization of fixed assets.

**Total Asset Turnover Ratio:** This ratio measures how effectively a company uses all its assets to generate sales.

| Ratio                       | Formula                                                             | Statement used |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------- |
| Inventory Turnover Ratio    | $\dfrac{\text{Cost of Goods Sold}}{\text{Average Inventory}}$       |                |
| Accounts Recievable Turover | $\dfrac{\text{Net Credit Sales}}{\text{Avg. Accounts Recievables}}$ |                |
| Fixed Assets Turnover Ratio | $\dfrac{\text{Net Sales}}{\text{Average Fixed Assets}}$             |                |
| Total Asset Turnover Ratio  | $\dfrac{\text{Net Sales}}{\text{Avergae Total Assets}}$             |                |
| Debtors Turnover Ratio      | $\dfrac{\text{Net Credit Sales}}{\text{Average Recievables}}$       |                |

 #### 4. Profitability Ratios
These ratios measure a company's profitability in relation to its sales, assets, or equity.
- Profitability of the company is the net result of its policies and decisions.
- These are used to
    - assess the adequacy of the profits earned by the company.
    - estimate the trend of profitability over a period of time.
- These ratios shows the combined effect of capital budgeting, liquidity management, assets management on operating results.
- These are measured with reference to sales, capital employed, total assets. shareholders funds, etc.

- **Gross Profit Margin:** This ratio shows the percentage of revenue remaining after deducting the cost of goods sold.
    
- **Net Profit Margin:** This ratio shows the percentage of revenue remaining after deducting all expenses, including taxes.
    
- **Return on Assets (ROA):** This ratio measures a company's profitability relative to its total assets.
    
- **Return on Capital Employed (ROCE):** Similar to ROA, but it focuses on the return generated on the capital employed in the business, which includes both debt and equity.
    
- **Return on Equity (ROE):** This ratio measures a company's profitability relative to its shareholders' equity. A higher ROE generally indicates better profitability for shareholders.

| Ratio                             | Formula                                                                   | Financial Statement used |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Gross Profit Margin               | $\dfrac{\text{Gross Profit}}{\text{Net Sales}} \times 100$                |                          |
| Net Profit Margin                 | $\dfrac{Net Profit}{Net Sales} \times 100$                                |                          |
| Return on Assets (ROA)            | $\dfrac{\text{Profit after Tax}}{\text{Average Total Assets}} \times 100$ |                          |
| Return on Capital Employed (ROCE) | $\dfrac{EBIT}{\text{Capital Employed}}$                                   |                          |
| Return on Equity (ROE)            | $\dfrac{\text{Equity Earnings}}{\text{Average Equity}} \times 100$        |                          |

### 5. Market Test Ratios
These ratios relate a company's stock price to its earnings, book value, or dividends.
- These relate to a firm’s stock to its earning or book value per share.
- These are indicator of performance and also reflect the likely performance of the company in near future.
- Market ratios are high, if company’s profitability, solvency and turnover ratios are good.

 **Price-Earnings Ratio (P/E Ratio):** This ratio compares a company's stock price to its earnings per share (EPS). A higher P/E ratio often indicates that investors are willing to pay a premium for the company's earnings growth potential.
 
**Dividend Payout Ratio:** This ratio shows the proportion of earnings paid out as dividends to shareholders.

| Ratio                            | Formula                                                              | Statement Used |
| -------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------- |
| Price-Earnings (P/E) Ratio       | $\dfrac{\text{Market Price per Share}}{\text{Earnings Per Share}}$   |                |
| Divident Payout Ratio            | $\dfrac{\text{Dividend Per Share}}{\text{Earnings Per Share}}$       |                |
| Market Value to Book Value Ratio | $\dfrac{\text{Market Price per Share}}{\text{Book price per share}}$ |                |

 
It's important to remember that these are just a few of the many financial ratios used in financial analysis. The specific ratios that are most important will vary depending on the industry, the company, and the specific needs of the analyst.

## Utility of Ratio Analysis

Ratios, by establishing mathematical relationships between different financial variables, unlock a wealth of information that might not be immediately apparent from raw financial data.

- Ratios are one of the most widely used tools of financial analysis.
- Ratios are used to determine various financial characteristics of a firm such as — growth prospect of the firm, financial & operational efficiency.
- Ratios can help determine
    - ability of a firm to service its current obligations.
    - effect of borrowing on long term solvency.
    - efficiency with which the firm is utilizing its assets in generating sales & revenues.
    - overall operating efficiency and performance of the firm.

## Diagnostic Role of Ratios

Beyond simply presenting financial data in a simplified form, ratios play a crucial diagnostic role in financial analysis. They act like financial health indicators, helping analysts identify areas of strength, pinpoint potential weaknesses, and understand the underlying causes of a company's financial performance. 

The diagnostic nature of ratios allows analysts to move beyond merely identifying symptoms to understanding the root causes of a company's financial performance. This deeper understanding is essential for making informed investment decisions, assessing creditworthiness, and identifying areas for operational improvement within a company.
